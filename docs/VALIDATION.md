# Validation

Every number here comes from a file in [`cuda/results/`](../cuda/results/), produced by `bash cuda/tools/reproduce_all.sh` on the machine in [MACHINE.md](../cuda/results/MACHINE.md) (NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 Laptop GPU). The complete tables, including run facts such as step counts and mass drift, are in [tables.md](../cuda/results/tables.md), generated by `cuda/tools/report.py`. The reasoning behind the setup is in [DESIGN_NOTES.md](DESIGN_NOTES.md).

## The benchmark

Schäfer and Turek (1996) define laminar flow around a cylinder in a channel: 2.2 m by 0.41 m, a cylinder of diameter D = 0.1 m at (0.2, 0.2), a parabolic inlet, no-slip walls and ν = 0.001 m²/s. Case 2D-1 has Re = 20 and a steady wake. Case 2D-2 has Re = 100 and periodic vortex shedding. The compared quantities are the drag and lift coefficients cD and cL, the Strouhal number St and the pressure difference Δp between the front and back of the cylinder.

<p align="center"><img src="../cuda/results/figures/setup.png" width="100%" alt="Benchmark geometry"></p>

The reference values for 2D-1 are the ones on the FeatFlow 2D-1 page (Nabh 1998). For 2D-2 they're extracted from the FeatFlow 2014 official results, finest level (Q2/P1disc, level 6, k = 1/1600), by `cuda/tools/extract_reference.py` into [extracted_2d2_reference.json](../cuda/reference/extracted_2d2_reference.json). The paper's acceptance ranges are from its Tables 3 and 4.

## Results at the finest grid

**2D-2, Re = 100, 160 cells per diameter** (3521 × 658 nodes), evaluated over the last three shedding periods after 27 consistent periods ([summary.json](../cuda/results/2D-2_N160_fp32/summary.json)):

| Quantity | Measured | Reference | Error | Paper range |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| cD max | 3.2476 | 3.2274 | +0.62 % | 3.22 to 3.24 (outside by 0.0076) |
| cL max | 1.0015 | 0.98658 | +1.51 % | 0.99 to 1.01 (inside) |
| St | 0.3017 | 0.30184 | −0.05 % | 0.295 to 0.305 (inside) |
| Δp(t0 + T/2) | 2.4841 | 2.4852 | −0.04 % | 2.46 to 2.50 (inside) |

The last two errors are at the resolution of the measurement. St from the last 3, 4 or 5 periods varies between −0.05 % and −0.06 %, and shifting the time of the lift maximum by one force sample (0.02 D/U) moves Δp(t0 + T/2) by about 0.03 %.

**2D-1, Re = 20, 80 cells per diameter** ([summary.json](../cuda/results/2D-1_N80_fp32/summary.json)). The values are time means over the last 20 D/U; see the limitations below for why.

| Quantity | Measured | Reference | Error | Paper range |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| cD | 5.6143 | 5.5795 | +0.62 % | 5.57 to 5.59 (outside by 0.024) |
| cL | 0.010934 | 0.010619 | +2.97 % | 0.0104 to 0.0110 (inside) |
| Δp | 0.11659 | 0.11752 | −0.80 % | 0.1172 to 0.1176 (outside by 0.0006) |

Where the finest grid falls outside the paper range, the reason is the staircase cylinder. A staircase boundary is first-order accurate, the drag error has the same sign on every grid, and the observed orders for the drag are about 1.5. So the remaining +0.6 % at 80 and 160 cells per diameter is the expected residual of that first-order term. The 2D-1 pressure difference stops improving between 40 and 80 cells (−0.98 %, then −0.80 %). The probe points lie on the cylinder surface, where the staircase shifts by a fraction of a cell between grids, so this error is most likely set by the geometry rather than the flow.

| 2D-1, cells per diameter | cD | cL | Δp |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 | 5.8792 (+5.37 %) | 0.01122 (+5.66 %) | 0.11843 (+0.77 %) |
| 40 | 5.6623 (+1.48 %) | 0.010596 (−0.21 %) | 0.11636 (−0.98 %) |

The errors fall with refinement for every 2D-2 quantity and for the 2D-1 drag. The fitted orders are 1.3 to 1.6 for the force peaks, which fits a staircase cylinder. In 2D-1 the lift and the pressure difference aren't monotone: the lift is a tiny by-product of the geometry (0.2 % of the drag), and both errors are already at the level the staircase position sets.

The 2D-2 stop rule (at least 12 consistent periods, last three within 0.2 %) was met on all three grids, after 13, 17 and 27 periods. Over the last 12 periods the spread is larger: 1.4 %, 0.7 % and 0.3 %.

### Richardson extrapolation and grid convergence index

From the three finest grids of each case, with refinement ratio r = 2 and safety factor 1.25 (Roache 1994; procedure as in Celik et al. 2008). The observed order p comes from the three values; "oscillatory" means the differences between grids change sign, in which case the GCI isn't a reliable bound.

| Case | Quantity | Finest grid | Richardson value | Its error vs reference | p | GCI fine | Convergence |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2D-2 | cD max | 3.24755 | 3.19433 | −1.02 % | 1.00 | 2.05 % | monotonic |
| 2D-2 | cL max | 1.00147 | 0.997368 | +1.09 % | 2.16 | 0.51 % | monotonic |
| 2D-2 | St | 0.301696 | 0.30555 | +1.23 % | 0.17 | 1.60 % | monotonic |
| 2D-2 | Δp(t0 + T/2) | 2.48409 | 2.48633 | +0.05 % | 2.65 | 0.11 % | oscillatory |
| 2D-1 | cD | 5.61432 | 5.60066 | +0.38 % | 2.17 | 0.30 % | monotonic |
| 2D-1 | cL | 0.0109341 | 0.0113333 | +6.73 % | 0.88 | 4.56 % | oscillatory |
| 2D-1 | Δp | 0.116585 | 0.116612 | −0.77 % | 3.22 | 0.03 % | oscillatory |

The 2D-1 drag is the clean case: monotonic, p = 2.17, and the extrapolated value is within 0.4 % of the reference. For the 2D-2 drag peak the reference lies inside the GCI band of the finest grid. St converges so slowly between 80 and 160 cells (p = 0.17) that its Richardson value overshoots; its finest-grid error of −0.05 % is already at the measurement resolution, so the extrapolation adds nothing there. The oscillatory quantities show that three grids aren't enough to extrapolate them.

## Plane Poiseuille verification

Before any cylinder run, the channel without the cylinder was compared with the analytic parabola at three heights ([poiseuille_summary.json](../cuda/results/poiseuille_summary.json)):

| H (cells) | L2 error, single precision | L2 error, double precision |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 41 | 4.405e-3 | 4.404e-3 |
| 82 | 1.088e-3 | 1.088e-3 |
| 164 | 2.726e-4 | 2.712e-4 |

The pairwise orders are 2.02 and 2.00, as expected for halfway bounce-back. The acceptance limits were an error below 1 % at H = 82 and an order between 1.8 and 2.2.

The single-precision runs at H = 82 and 164 end at their time limit of 20 diffusion times, because the profile change stays at the single-precision noise floor just above the 1e-6 tolerance. The double-precision runs reach exactly zero change and give the same errors.

This check failed at first. With plain populations in single precision the finest channel never settled, because the viscous stress is a difference of order 1e-5 between numbers of order 0.4. Storing the deviation from the rest state instead fixed it (Skordos 1993). The traces of the failing and the fixed runs are in [poiseuille_investigation/](../cuda/results/poiseuille_investigation/README.md).

## Single versus double precision

Double-precision builds were run for 2D-1 at 40 and 80 cells per diameter and 2D-2 at 80 cells per diameter. The largest FP32 to FP64 difference is 5.5e-4 in the 2D-2 lift peak, which is 4 % of the change between the two finest grids; every other difference is below 2 % of that change. So single-precision storage isn't a limiting error on these grids.

One effect remains. In single precision the steady 2D-1 drag keeps a small ripple of 1e-4 to 1e-3 relative, while the double-precision run at 40 cells settles below the 1e-4 band. The time means agree to 2e-5 in cD.

## The demo reproduces the benchmark

The demo runs the same kernels, case and sampling interval as the benchmark. Run headless for the step count of the 80-cell 2D-2 benchmark, it reproduces cD max, cL max and St to 1e-4 % ([consistency.json](../cuda/results/demo_consistency/consistency.json)). This is an equality check of two drivers over shared code: it guarantees that what the window shows is what was validated, but it can't catch a bug they share.

## Limitations

- **2D-1 runs at 40 and 80 cells hit the time limit.** A small sustained ripple keeps the drag band above the 1e-4 stop criterion, so these runs end at 400 D/U. The reported values are 20 D/U time means; the last samples are stored next to them in each summary.json (`cd_last`, `cl_last`, `dp_last`).
- **The 2D-1 lift depends on the averaging window.** cD and Δp are robust: the cD mean over any 20 D/U window from 80 to 400 D/U is within 4e-5. The lift mean at 80 cells moves by about ±0.15 % with the window, because the ripple is 31 % of the small lift value.
- **Double precision was run on three grids only:** 2D-1 at 40 and 80 cells and 2D-2 at 80 cells, plus the Poiseuille channels.
- **The 2D-1 case at 160 cells per diameter wasn't run.** It would take about 1.9 hours, because the band criterion doesn't fire in single precision and the run would go to the 400 D/U limit.
- **The outlet is a fixed-pressure condition,** not the paper's "do nothing" outflow. Pressure waves reflect between inlet and outlet and decay slowly, which is why the periodic runs need at least 12 consistent periods before evaluation.
- **BGK at Re = 100 runs at tau = 0.56,** close to the stability limit of 0.5 (Krüger et al. 2017, ch. 4). The demo's other-Reynolds-number mode therefore runs only up to Re 110 ([showcase_check](../cuda/results/showcase_check/)).
